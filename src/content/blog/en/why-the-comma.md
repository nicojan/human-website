The name of this collective is "Human,". With the comma.

I get asked about the punctuation every time I write the name somewhere new. Most people assume it's a typo. A few assume it's a design affectation. Neither is quite right, so I've started to explain.

A comma is a pause, not a stop. It says: more is coming. Something else belongs next to this word. When I write "Human," and nothing else follows, the comma is doing a small but specific thing. It's leaving room.

That's the feeling I want this practice to have. Being human isn't a finished thing you learn about once in kindergarten. It's an opening. A comma. Whatever comes next depends on what we do with the time, the texts, and the questions in front of us.

There's a more practical reason too. I started teaching when most of my students could still Google their way into a decent essay. Now any of them can ask an AI to write the whole thing. The essay arrives in seconds, looking plausible. Grammar checks out. Vocabulary is adult. It also says nothing.

The gap between a machine essay and a human essay used to be about skill. It isn't anymore. The machine can write a skilful essay. What it can't do is mean anything. It can't tell you why a particular passage made a student's hand shake the first time she read it. It can't sit with a question for a week. It can't decide that the thesis it had yesterday was wrong and the new one is closer to the truth.

Those are the parts I want to protect. The pause. The sitting with. The decision that something mattered.

So: Human,. A comma. An opening for whatever a student brings next.

If you're reading this, thank you. Something about the name made you curious enough to click. That's already the habit I'm trying to help students keep.
