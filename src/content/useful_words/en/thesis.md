---
term: Thesis
pronunciation: /ˈθiː.sɪs/
definition: >-
  The argument a piece of writing is trying to make. A strong thesis is specific
  enough to be argued against. If nobody could disagree with it, it isn't a
  thesis yet; it's a summary.
example: >-
  Weak: 'Hamlet is a complex play.' Strong: 'Hamlet is a play about the cost of
  demanding certainty before acting.'
etymology: >-
  From Greek thesis, meaning 'a placing' or 'a setting down.' The word carries
  the sense of putting a stake in the ground.
lang: en
pair_key: thesis
humanized_sha: 92d7e14d80840d752885576a39e940b7eabcadb0995978c1a448128e8b6a0cd5
---

In our lessons, students practise revising weak theses into strong ones. Usually the fix is to replace a feature of the text ("the imagery is striking") with a claim about the text ("the imagery convinces us that the narrator is unreliable").
