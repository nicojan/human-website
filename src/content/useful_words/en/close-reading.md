---
term: Close reading
pronunciation: /kloʊs ˈriː.dɪŋ/
definition: >-
  The practice of slowing down in front of a text until you start noticing
  things you'd miss at regular speed: word choice, rhythm, what's left unsaid.
  In class it usually means marking up a passage together, sentence by sentence.
example: >-
  A close reading of the first paragraph showed that every sentence used a form
  of the verb 'to be' except the last, which snapped into the active voice.
etymology: >-
  From the New Critics of the mid-20th century who insisted that a poem or
  passage could be understood on its own terms before any biographical or
  historical context was added.
lang: en
pair_key: close-reading
humanized_sha: 6a28ac48d9a254322d3e42e71d52b63293053b5f1f1a43a81142cecaa148926c
---

In our lessons, a close reading is usually a shared-document exercise. We pick a short passage, highlight moves that stand out, and write a one-sentence reaction in the margin. The reactions become the seeds of essays.
