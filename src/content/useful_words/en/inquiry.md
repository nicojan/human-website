---
term: Inquiry
pronunciation: /ɪnˈkwaɪə.ri/
definition: >-
  Asking questions that open the conversation instead of closing it.
  Inquiry-based learning means the student's questions drive the lesson. A good
  inquiry question has no obvious answer, and a boring answer feels wrong.
example: >-
  Closed question: 'Is the narrator unreliable?' Inquiry question: 'What does
  the narrator want us to believe, and why should we be suspicious of that?'
etymology: >-
  From Latin inquirere, 'to seek into.' The preposition matters: you're looking
  into something, not at it.
lang: en
pair_key: inquiry
humanized_sha: fb7201f1df08269fdd71db27e3e425eed1c1f5716b2865a472388696a87ae283
---

We build lessons around inquiry questions because the alternative (quizzing students on what the teacher already knows) is a tired format that doesn't teach anyone to think. An inquiry question is the same question the teacher is still chewing on.
